# Research translated into this build

This project follows the earlier complete TryMacApps review and the subsequent decision to build one integrated file product. It does not attempt to launch the earlier eight-tool bundle.

## The useful task

People collect attachments from several locations, prepare images for a recipient, and deliver them together. A shelf reduces juggling; preparation avoids opening separate converters; a clean ZIP provides a normal portable result. Our product packages these steps into one calm, visible workspace.

The signature demo is simple: drop pieces into an illustrated folder, prepare image copies, watch the cover close, then drag the completed package away. The ordinary ZIP is the output; a recipient needs no account or Parcel installation.

## Evidence and its limits

The prior research checked Dropover's official product/actions and App Store reviews, PackMint Desk's product listing, and Reddit file-utility discussions. That supported a paying niche and repeated everyday use. It also revealed direct overlap: ZIPs, manifests, image conversion/compression and shelves already exist. We should market a coherent experience and visual character, not falsely claim invention or exclusivity.

The FilePane alternatives discussion described friction around conversions/file handling, preserving originals and repeated save choices. This became prepared-copy state, explicit original/prepared comparison, and remembering the selected save destination. A single discussion is qualitative evidence, not a market-size estimate.

Launch pricing is $2.49 for one Mac, $4.99 for two Macs and $9.99 for five Macs, with no subscription or automatic trial. These low one-time prices are still a market hypothesis; useful repeat work and excellent interaction must earn the purchase. No competitor rating or adjacent revenue figure is projected into Parcel sales.

## What to learn from a real beta

Observe whether people repeatedly collect/prepare/send real work, whether the shelf makes those steps clearer, and where source/destination compatibility fails. Record waitlist-to-paid conversion and returns through the chosen merchant platform. Use that evidence to prioritize one additional utility at a time. The app currently contains no analytics SDK or automatic telemetry.
